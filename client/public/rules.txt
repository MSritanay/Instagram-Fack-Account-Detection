IMPORTANT: This is not legal advice. This is a simplified guide to help you stay safe. When in doubt, consult a legal professional.

The Indian government has strict laws to protect you from online fraud. Two of the most important are Sections 66C and 66D of the Information Technology Act. Understanding them is your first line of defense.

---

### SECTION 66C: PUNISHMENT FOR IDENTITY THEFT

This section deals with the fraudulent use of someone else's identity. It's about more than just passwords; it covers any unique identification feature.

**What is a Crime Under Section 66C?**

- **Using Electronic Signatures or Passwords:** Illegally using someone else's password, OTP (One-Time Password), or digital signature to log in or authorize something as them.
- **Using Biometric Data:** Unauthorized use of fingerprint, retina scan, or other biometric data.
- **Using any other "Unique Identification Feature":** This is a broad term that can include Aadhaar numbers, PAN cards, or even unique device IDs.

**The Core Idea:** If you pretend to be someone else online using their private information without their explicit permission, you are committing a crime.

### SECTION 66D: PUNISHMENT FOR CHEATING BY PERSONATION

This section targets the act of cheating someone by using a computer or other communication device to pretend to be someone else.

**What is a Crime Under Section 66D?**

- **Phishing:** Creating a fake website (e.g., a bank login page) to trick people into giving up their credentials.
- **Catfishing:** Creating a fake social media profile to deceive someone into a relationship or to extract money.
- **Impersonating a Company or Official:** Pretending to be from a well-known company (like Amazon or a bank) or a government agency to defraud someone.
- **Fake Job Offers:** Tricking people into paying for a job that doesn't exist.

**The Core Idea:** If you use a computer or the internet to create a false identity to deceive and cheat someone, you are committing a crime.

**Key Takeaways for Your Safety:**

1.  **NEVER Share Your Credentials:** Your passwords, OTPs, and PINs are like keys to your digital life. Never give them to anyone, no matter who they claim to be.
2.  **Be Skeptical of "Too Good to Be True" Offers:** Scammers often lure victims with promises of easy money, high returns, or exclusive deals. Always be suspicious.
3.  **Verify Identities:** If someone contacts you unexpectedly claiming to be from a bank or other institution, do not trust them outright. Hang up and contact the institution through their official, public channels.
4.  **Check URLs Carefully:** Before entering any login information, double-check the website's URL to ensure it's the legitimate site and not a phishing fake. Look for `https://` and a lock icon.
5.  **Think Before You Click:** Be wary of unsolicited links sent via email, text, or social media, even if they appear to be from someone you know.

By being aware of these rules and staying vigilant, you can significantly reduce your risk of falling victim to online scams.
